The morning light hit the waterfront cafe at that perfect angle—the kind that made Emma Chen squint as she settled into her corner table with her oat milk latte and the latest issue of *Architectural Digest*. She'd claimed this spot specifically because it faced away from the water, away from the distractions. She had blueprints to review, a preliminary site assessment due by noon, and approximately zero patience for anything that would derail her focus.

The blueprints were spread across the small marble-top table, weighted down by her phone and a sugar packet at the corners. Her fingers traced the load-bearing walls of the adaptive reuse project—a century-old warehouse in the Arts District that the developers wanted to transform into mixed-use residential and retail space. It was good work. Her work. She'd spent three weeks getting every detail right, and she wanted to catch any flaws before the client meeting.

She was so absorbed that she didn't notice the man approaching from her left until he was already there, sliding into the adjacent table with an armful of rolled papers and a coffee that smelled like it had been brewed in heaven.

Emma glanced up briefly, registered that he was handsome in that effortless way that suggested he didn't spend much time thinking about it, and returned her attention to her blueprints. The cafe was busy enough that shared tables were normal. She expected him to mind his own business.

He did, for approximately four minutes.

"Is that a warehouse conversion?" His voice was warm, curious, the kind of tone that suggested genuine interest rather than small talk.

Emma looked up again, this time taking him in properly. Dark hair, warm brown eyes, the kind of casual confidence that came from being comfortable in his own skin. He was gesturing toward her blueprints with his coffee cup, which was still dangerously full.

"Arts District project," she said, keeping it brief. "You in construction?"

"Restaurant owner." He smiled. "I'm Marcus. And I'm asking because I'm looking at opening a new place, and I've been obsessing over floor plans all morning. Thought maybe I recognized a kindred spirit."

Despite herself, Emma felt the corner of her mouth twitch upward. "Emma. And I appreciate the sentiment, but these are kind of a mess to explain right now—"

That was when Marcus's elbow caught the edge of his coffee cup.

It happened in that slow-motion, inevitable way that made Emma's stomach drop. The cup tipped. The lid—which apparently hadn't been secured properly—popped off. And what felt like an entire reservoir of dark roast coffee cascaded directly onto her blueprints.

For a moment, neither of them moved. The coffee spread across the paper like a dark stain, soaking into the lines she'd drawn so carefully, pooling in the corners of the architectural renderings.

"Oh my God," Marcus said, and he was already on his feet, already grabbing napkins from the dispenser at his table. "Oh my God, I am so sorry. I'm—Jesus, I'm so sorry."

Emma watched the coffee seep into the paper, watched her work become something abstract and ruined, and felt the laugh bubble up before she could stop it. It wasn't hysterical—it was more the laugh of someone who recognized absurdity when it was literally dripping onto her lap.

"It's fine," she said, and meant it, mostly. "These are just copies. The files are digital."

"It's not fine." Marcus was already at her table now, napkins in both hands, trying to blot the damage without making it worse. He was making it worse. "I'm a disaster. I'm actually a disaster. My staff tells me this constantly, and I never believe them, and then I do something like this."

Despite the chaos, Emma found herself smiling. There was something genuinely endearing about his panic, about the way he was treating her ruined blueprints like he'd committed a crime.

"Seriously," she said, reaching over to gently take some of the napkins from his hands. "It's okay. Occupational hazard of working in cafes, I guess. I should have known better."

"No, I should have known better." Marcus stepped back, running a hand through his hair. He looked genuinely distressed. "I'm the one who knocked over the coffee. I'm the one who wasn't paying attention. I was too busy being interested in—" He stopped, seemed to realize what he was about to say, and a flush crept up his neck. "—in architectural design. Which is apparently a hazardous hobby for me."

Emma looked at him then, really looked at him. There was something charming about his mortification, something real about the way he wasn't trying to make a joke of it or brush it off. He actually cared that he'd made a mess.

"You know what?" she said, making a decision that surprised herself. "You can buy me a new coffee, and we can call it even."

Marcus's expression shifted, hope flickering across his face. "Yeah?"

"Yeah. And maybe you can show me these floor plans you've been obsessing over. Since you apparently think we're kindred spirits."

He grinned then, and it transformed his face entirely—less polished, more genuine. "I'm going to need to buy you more than one coffee to make up for this. But I'll take it."

While he went to order—insisting on paying for Emma's replacement latte and adding a croissant to the order, which she didn't protest—Emma gathered the ruined blueprints and set them aside. She pulled out her phone and brought up the digital files instead, arranging them on the table so they'd be visible when he returned.

When Marcus came back with the drinks and pastries, he unrolled his own plans carefully, as if the blueprints themselves might be fragile. They were good—she could see that immediately. The layout for a farm-to-table restaurant, thoughtfully designed, with an open kitchen concept and a wine bar that looked like it would actually work functionally. There was care in every measurement, intention in every space.

"This is really good," she said, and meant it.

"Yeah?" He looked pleased, then sheepish. "I had some help from a designer friend, but I wanted to make sure the vision was actually feasible before we went too far down the road."

"The vision is sound. The execution needs some tweaking—your wine service area is going to create a bottleneck during service, and your storage is undersized—but the bones are there."

Marcus leaned forward, genuinely interested, and as Emma found herself walking him through the issues and potential solutions, she realized that the ruined blueprints didn't matter anymore. The coffee stain had been worth it, just for this—for the way his eyes lit up when she explained a solution, for the way he listened like what she was saying actually mattered.

The morning light shifted across the table, and Emma thought that maybe accidentally spilling coffee on someone was a pretty good way to meet them after all.
