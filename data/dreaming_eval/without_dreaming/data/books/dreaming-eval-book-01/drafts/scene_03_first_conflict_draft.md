The coffee shop smelled like cinnamon and regret. Emma was halfway through her latte when she saw him—David, standing in the doorway like a character who'd wandered into the wrong scene. Her stomach dropped. The cup froze at her lips.

He looked different. Older maybe, or just worn in a way she didn't remember. His hair was shorter. He'd always talked about cutting it. She'd always talked him out of it.

"Emma." Just her name, but it carried the weight of three years. The weight of a ring she'd stopped wearing six months before she took it off.

"David." She set the cup down with deliberate care, buying time she didn't have. "What are you doing in Portland?"

He moved toward her table like he had every right to. He probably thought he did. "I'm in town for a conference. Saw you through the window." He smiled—that smile that used to make her feel like the luckiest person alive. Now it made her feel trapped. "Can I sit?"

She nodded because refusing would create a scene, and Emma had spent the last two years learning to avoid scenes. She was good at it. Polite refusals. Careful words. Nothing that would require her to actually feel anything.

"You look good," David said, settling into the chair across from her. "Happy."

The word landed like a challenge. Was she happy? She'd been thinking she was. Marcus had made her feel things she'd forgotten existed—lightness, spontaneity, the kind of desire that made her forget to check her phone. But looking at David now, at the tangible evidence of her past failure, happiness seemed like a precarious thing. A house built on sand.

"I'm doing well," she said, which wasn't the same thing.

"I heard you were seeing someone." He said it carefully, like he was testing the waters. "Marcus, right? That's... that's good. I'm glad."

But his jaw tightened when he said it, and Emma remembered that tell. David had never been good at hiding things, even when he tried. It was one of the reasons she'd loved him once. It was one of the reasons she'd left him.

"How long?" he asked.

"Three months."

"That's still new."

"It is," she agreed, and something in her chest twisted. Three months. It was new. What did she know about Marcus, really? She knew how he kissed. She knew he made terrible jokes and laughed at his own punchlines. She knew he wanted things from her—commitment, honesty, the kind of vulnerability that had destroyed her before. She knew she was terrified of giving him any of it.

"Listen," David leaned forward. "I know I don't have the right, but I need to say this. I made mistakes. A lot of them. But I've been in therapy, and I've done a lot of work, and I think—I think I was just too young. We were too young. But now..." He trailed off, and Emma felt the walls she'd built start to crack. "I think I could be what you needed. What you deserve."

The words hung between them like a noose.

"David, don't."

"I'm not asking you to choose right now. I'm just asking you to think about it. To consider that maybe we didn't fail. Maybe we were just ahead of ourselves."

Emma's phone buzzed. A text from Marcus: *Still on for dinner tonight? Really want to see you.*

She stared at it, unable to move.

"Who is it?" David asked, and she hated that he could read her so easily.

"No one. I mean—Marcus."

"You should answer him."

She should. She didn't. Instead, she set the phone face-down on the table and watched it buzz again. And again.

"I can't do this," she said finally.

"Do what? Talk?"

"Any of it." She stood up, gathering her things with shaking hands. "You can't just show up and—and rewrite the ending. That's not how this works."

"I know. I'm sorry. I just needed you to know that I'm different now. That I'm ready."

But Emma wasn't thinking about David being ready. She was thinking about Marcus, who'd asked her last week if she could see a future with him. She'd deflected with humor. He hadn't pushed, but she'd seen something flicker in his eyes—disappointment, maybe. Or fear. The fear that she was already halfway out the door.

"I have to go," she said.

She left David sitting alone at the table, her untouched latte slowly cooling beside him.

---

Marcus was already at the restaurant when she arrived, and he knew immediately that something was wrong. She could see it in the way his face changed when he looked at her—the careful smile that didn't reach his eyes.

"Hey," he said, standing to kiss her cheek. "You okay?"

"Fine. Just a long day."

He sat back down, studying her. "You seem off."

"I'm not." She picked up the menu, hiding behind it. "I'm just tired."

"Emma." He waited until she lowered the menu. "Talk to me. Please."

She wanted to. She wanted to tell him about David, about the way seeing him had cracked open something she'd sealed shut. She wanted to explain that it wasn't about David—it was about her, about the fact that she'd failed at forever once and she was terrified of failing again. She wanted to be brave enough to let Marcus in.

Instead, she said, "I'm not sure I'm ready for what you want."

She watched his face carefully. Watched him process it. Watched him try to hide the hurt.

"Okay," he said quietly. "What do I want?"

"Commitment. A future. All of it."

"And you're not ready for that."

"I don't know if I'm ready for that. With anyone."

Marcus set his menu down. He looked at her for a long moment, and she could see him building walls of his own, brick by careful brick.

"Then I guess we have a problem," he said. "Because I am."

The restaurant continued around them—other people's laughter, the clink of glasses, the hum of normal life. Emma reached for his hand, but he'd already pulled away.

"I don't know what to do here," she whispered.

"Neither do I," Marcus said. And that was the truth that terrified her most—that for the first time since they'd met, they were completely lost.
